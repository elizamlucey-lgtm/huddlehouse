# Customer Feedback and Issue Triage

Customer feedback helps us understand where people need more support.

A question that appears small to one person may reveal a missing instruction, confusing workflow, or product issue affecting many customers.

Nova and Pip help the team turn feedback into useful improvements.

## 1. Where feedback comes from

Feedback may come from:

- Customer Support conversations
- Customer Success meetings
- Product feedback
- Engineering investigations
- Community discussions
- Documentation comments
- Reported product issues

Record the problem clearly and preserve relevant context.

## 2. What makes useful feedback?

A useful report explains:

| Information | Question |
|---|---|
| Customer goal | What was the customer trying to accomplish? |
| Problem | What happened? |
| Expected result | What did the customer expect? |
| Actual result | What happened instead? |
| Environment | Which product version or environment was involved? |
| Evidence | Are there approved logs, examples, or screenshots? |

Do not include sensitive customer information in shared channels.

## 3. Triage the issue

Not every problem is a documentation problem.

Use these categories to decide what needs attention.

| Category | Description | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Documentation gap | Required information is missing | Create a documentation task |
| Unclear instructions | Existing guidance is difficult to follow | Review and revise the content |
| Product issue | Product behavior is incorrect or unexpected | Refer to Product or Engineering |
| Documentation defect | Existing content is inaccurate | Verify and correct the content |
| Feature request | Customer wants new functionality | Share with Product |
| User education | Customer needs help understanding a workflow | Consider a guide or training resource |

## 4. The feedback workflow

1. Receive and record the feedback.
2. Identify the customer's goal.
3. Categorize the issue.
4. Gather supporting information.
5. Ask the appropriate team to verify the behavior.
6. Decide whether documentation changes are needed.
7. Create a task in Projects when follow-up work is required.
8. Review and publish approved updates.
9. Share the outcome with the relevant teams.

## 5. Example: A missing installation prerequisite

**Customer report**

A customer followed the installation guide but could not complete the setup.

**Initial investigation**

Support discovers that the customer did not configure a required connection profile.

**Documentation review**

The writer checks the guide and finds that the prerequisite is missing.

**Engineering verification**

Engineering confirms the correct setup sequence and provides an approved example.

**Documentation update**

The writer adds:

- The missing prerequisite
- The correct sequence of actions
- An example command
- An expected result
- A troubleshooting note

**Follow-up**

The writer creates a documentation task, completes the review, and shares the update with Support.

## 6. When to use AI during triage

AI may help organize feedback by:

- Summarizing approved feedback
- Identifying recurring themes
- Grouping similar questions
- Suggesting potential documentation gaps
- Drafting a list of investigation questions

AI must not decide that a product is defective or invent the cause of a reported problem.

A person must investigate and verify the issue.

## 7. What does success look like?

A good feedback process should help the team:

- Reduce repeated customer confusion
- Improve the accuracy of documentation
- Identify product friction
- Make important information easier to find
- Share verified improvements with the right people

> Pip's reminder: Don't just close the conversation. Close the loop. Make sure the person who reported the problem knows what happened next.
