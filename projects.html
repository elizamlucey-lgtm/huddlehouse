The documentation delivery workflow

Clear checkpoints help the team catch problems before customers do.

1

Draft

Gather requirements, approved source information, audience needs, and examples.

2

Technical review

Ask Engineering or the appropriate SME to verify product behavior and technical details.

3

Editorial review

Check structure, terminology, clarity, accessibility, and style-guide consistency.

4

QA and validation

Test procedures, commands, links, code examples, and expected results.

5

Publish

Merge the approved change, publish the update, and communicate what changed.

Part 01 · Prompt engineering

The AI prompt library

Different documentation challenges need different instructions. Each prompt includes a specific purpose, review criteria, and boundaries.

⌘
📖 Editorial quality

Style and consistency

Identify inconsistent terminology, unclear sentences, formatting issues, and potential style-guide violations.

Explore this prompt

Compare the supplied draft against the approved style guide. Identify each potential issue, explain the rule, and suggest a revision. Do not change technical meaning or invent facts.

Best for: editorial reviews and documentation maintenance.
🔎 Completeness

Content gap analysis

Find missing prerequisites, permissions, steps, expected results, and troubleshooting information.

Explore this prompt

Review the document from the perspective of a first-time user. Identify missing information, explain why it matters, and list questions for the product team.

Best for: user guides, tutorials, and onboarding.
🛡️ Technical quality

Technical accuracy

Compare documentation against approved source material and flag unsupported or conflicting statements.

Explore this prompt

Compare the draft only against the supplied specification, test results, or SME notes. Identify discrepancies and request confirmation where evidence is missing.

Best for: API references, CLI instructions, and configuration guides.
💬 Reader experience

Plain language and usability

Find confusing explanations, unexplained terms, dense paragraphs, and instructions that are difficult to follow.

Explore this prompt

Evaluate the draft for a defined audience. Identify language that may confuse a reader and suggest clearer alternatives without removing necessary technical detail.

Best for: onboarding, help content, and tutorials.
📣 Product communication

Release communications

Check whether release notes explain the change, customer impact, required actions, and supporting resources.

Explore this prompt

Review release notes against approved release details. Identify missing context, unclear impact, and questions for Product or Engineering.

Best for: release notes and customer announcements.
☑️ Quality assurance

Final documentation QA

Inspect structure, links, examples, accessibility considerations, and publication readiness.

Explore this prompt

Use a defined checklist to inspect the supplied document. Separate verifiable findings from items that require a human or technical review.

Best for: pre-publication reviews and recurring quality checks.

● Critical gap Pending
Prerequisites are not defined

The draft mentions administrator access but does not explain the actual requirements.

Why does this matter?

Reader impact: A user may begin installation without the permissions or dependencies needed to complete it.

Suggested action: Ask Engineering to confirm supported environments, dependencies, and required permissions before adding instructions.

AI limitation: AI cannot determine the real product requirements from this draft alone.

● Technical question Pending
Command behavior needs verification

The document provides a command but does not explain its required arguments or expected behavior.

Why does this matter?

Reader impact: Users may not know whether the command is complete, what it changes, or what output to expect.

Suggested action: Request an approved command example and expected output from Engineering.

AI limitation: A plausible-looking command is not proof that it works.

● Content gap Pending
Configuration steps are missing

The draft says configuration must be completed but does not explain how to complete it.

Why does this matter?

Reader impact: The user is told to complete an action without receiving the information needed to do it.

Suggested action: Obtain the approved configuration workflow, required values, and expected result.

AI limitation: AI can identify the missing explanation, but cannot safely invent the workflow.

● Usability issue Pending
Troubleshooting guidance is too broad

“Check your settings or contact support” does not give the reader a useful diagnostic path.

Why does this matter?

Reader impact: Users are left without a clear next step when something goes wrong.

Suggested action: Identify common failure messages, diagnostic checks, recovery steps, and escalation details with Support.

AI limitation: AI can suggest a clearer structure but needs verified troubleshooting information.

● Editorial suggestion Pending
Some wording is vague

Expressions such as “everything is configured” and “if it doesn't work” could be more specific.

Why does this matter?

Reader impact: Vague language can make it difficult to understand when a step is complete or what to do next.

Suggested action: Define the expected result and use specific, observable conditions where possible.

AI limitation: Suggested wording still needs to preserve the verified product behavior.

Part 03 · Practical impact

A little more clarity goes a long way

AI can suggest ways to improve readability. The writer must still verify that the revised instructions are accurate.

✨
Before · Original draft
Once everything is configured, start the service. If it doesn't work, check your settings or contact support.

The instruction is vague. It does not explain how to confirm configuration, how to start the service, or what to do when an error occurs.

After · Improved structure
1. Complete the required configuration steps.
2. Verify that the configuration check completes successfully.
3. Start the service using the approved command.
4. Confirm that the service reports a successful startup.

This example illustrates a clearer structure. The actual commands, configuration steps, and success messages must be supplied and verified by the product team.

Part 04 · Responsible AI

The human review checkpoint

AI can help find potential issues. It cannot approve product behavior or take responsibility for published documentation.

🤝
1. Give AI boundaries Supply approved source material, define the audience, and provide clear instructions. Protect confidential information.
2. Evaluate the output Check false positives, missed issues, unsupported claims, and whether suggestions preserve the original meaning.
3. Keep human approval Writers make editorial decisions. SMEs verify technical details. Humans approve publication.
👽
Nova and Pip's big takeaway

AI can help us spot the things we might miss. But a good technical writer knows which suggestions to accept, which to question, and when to bring in an expert. That's how we turn AI assistance into trustworthy documentation.
